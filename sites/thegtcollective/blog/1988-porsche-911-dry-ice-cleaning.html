Case Study / Dry Ice Cleaning

A clearer view of an air-cooled icon.

A preservation-first dry ice cleaning of a 1988 Porsche 911, documented from intake through final delivery.

By Mike Zanni July 26, 2026 6 minute read
Vehicle1988 Porsche 911
ServiceDry Ice Cleaning
Primary focusUndercarriage
StudioLeonardtown, MD

A 38-year-old Porsche should not look new underneath. My job was to remove what did not belong so the car's real condition was easier to see.

This 1988 Porsche 911 came down from the Baltimore area to my studio in Leonardtown for a careful cleaning underneath. Nearly four decades of use had left buildup across the lower structure and suspension, hiding the surfaces and finishes below.

On a collector car, cleaning can turn into alteration faster than people think. I used dry ice so I could work in small, controlled areas without introducing water or leaving blasting media behind.

I photographed each area before and after the work. Bryan now has a cleaner underside and a useful record of what is actually there.

The method

Controlled cleaning, zone by zone.

Dry ice cleaning works because the solid CO2 media strikes the contamination and sublimates into gas. There is no wash water and no blasting media left behind. The dislodged material still has to be managed, and the operator still has to make careful decisions around every surface.

Document

Record the condition and establish repeatable views before cleaning begins.

Calibrate

Adjust pressure, media flow, angle, and distance to the material in front of the nozzle.

Work by zone

Move methodically through the underbody, suspension areas, and adjacent components.

Record again

Photograph the same areas after cleaning so the work can be evaluated honestly.

What the result means

A clean surface is information.

On an older Porsche, the value of dry ice cleaning is not a manufactured “like new” finish. It is the ability to see and document the car with fewer layers of contamination in the way.

  • Original finishes, coatings, and visible age remain part of the car.
  • Surface condition becomes easier to inspect, photograph, and discuss.
  • Matched before-and-after views create a useful record for the owner.
  • The approach can be adjusted around rubber, wiring, labels, and older materials.
