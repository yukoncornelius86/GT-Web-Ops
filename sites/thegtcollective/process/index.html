The process · Consult to delivery

A clear beginning. A defined finish line.

Seven stages keep the work aligned with the vehicle, the stated purpose, and the scope you approved.

01—07
The working sequence

The vehicle moves forward only when the decision is clear.

The timeline is deliberate without being rigid. Condition can change the method or pace; it does not silently change the agreement.

01

Preservation consultation

We discuss the vehicle's history, current condition, your priorities, areas of concern, and what you want the finished work to accomplish.Output · Purpose statement

02

Condition review

The vehicle or supplied documentation is reviewed for access, materials, contamination, previous repairs, fragile areas, and signs that another specialist should be involved.Output · Initial fit and risk notes

03

Written preservation brief

The intended outcome, included zones, exclusions, test areas, documentation plan, acceptance criteria, and approval path are put in writing before scheduling.Output · Agreed project scope

04

Intake and baseline record

On arrival, we confirm condition and record repeatable views. Any meaningful difference from the brief is discussed before work begins.Output · Intake record and matched views

05

Calibration and controlled work

A discreet test area establishes the method. Work proceeds by zone using the least aggressive effective approach, with vulnerable items protected or excluded.Output · Zone-by-zone execution

06

Scope gate when needed

If a hidden condition, access issue, failed coating, or new opportunity changes the brief, work stops. You receive a photograph, explanation, and recommendation before choosing the next step.Output · Continue, revise, defer, or stop

07

Final record and delivery

We photograph the same areas, review the result against the brief, identify what remains, and provide care notes and future priorities without manufacturing more work.Output · Honest handoff

The scope gate

Nothing expands by assumption.

Pause

The affected zone stops before further intervention.

Explain

You see the condition, consequence, and practical choices.

Approve

The scope changes only after a clear decision from you.

Documentation is part of the work

Before and after should mean the same view.

A dramatic photograph can sell a transformation. A matched photograph can explain one.

Whenever access allows, the final record returns to the same components, orientation, and scale. The goal is comparison—not spectacle.

Review a documented project
Timing

We schedule around condition, not a package clock.

Access, contamination, material fragility, documentation, and approval gates affect the pace. The preservation brief establishes the expected window; meaningful changes are communicated instead of absorbed into rushed work.

  • Drop-off and collection windows are confirmed before arrival.
  • Additional time is not treated as automatic additional scope.
  • A delivery date moves only when the condition or your decision requires it.

Bring us the vehicle and the question you want answered.

The consultation turns that question into a purpose, a scope, and a finish line.
