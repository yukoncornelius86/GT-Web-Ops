Our position

Preservation is not less ambitious. It is more exact.

The point is not to do as little as possible. The point is to do exactly what the vehicle and the stated purpose justify.

A preserved surface may retain staining, tool marks, factory overspray, faded coatings, handwritten markings, or the quiet evidence of decades on the road. Those details can be information, not flaws to erase.

Clean enough to reveal the car.
Careful enough to preserve its story.

GT working principle

Five principles

The decisions behind the finish.

Reveal before deciding

Contamination can hide both excellent original condition and problems that deserve attention. We create clarity before recommending the next intervention.

Keep honest age

Original finish, sound coatings, labels, markings, patina, and visible use remain when removing them would trade history for temporary uniformity.

Use the least aggressive effective method

Pressure, chemistry, heat, abrasion, media, distance, and dwell time are choices—not defaults. The material in front of us sets the limit.

Document the judgment

Matched photographs and clear notes show what changed, what remained, and where the vehicle asked us to stop or involve another specialist.

Stop when value flattens

More effort is not automatically more care. We stop at the agreed finish line when additional work offers little meaningful benefit or adds avoidable risk.

Craft is judgment

The best decision may be to leave it alone.

Restraint is not hesitation. It is a practiced choice.

When a label is brittle, a coating is lifting, a fastener tells a story, or a surface cannot be improved without altering it, responsible work may protect, document, and move on.
